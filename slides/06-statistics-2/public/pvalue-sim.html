p-value under H0

Under $H_0$, the p-value is uniformly distributed on $[0, 1]$. That is the reason the decision rule "reject if $p \lt \alpha$" controls the Type I rate at exactly $\alpha$: a fraction $\alpha$ of p-values fall below $\alpha$, by construction.

data $X_1, \dots, X_n \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ (so $H_0: \mu = 0$ is true). Continuous data so that $Z$ and $p$ are continuous too.
statistic $Z = \sqrt{n}\,\bar X$, two-sided $p = 2(1 - \Phi(|Z|))$
claim deciles of $Z$ and deciles of $p$ both contain roughly $1/10$ of all draws under $H_0$
