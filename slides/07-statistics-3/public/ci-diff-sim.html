CI of the difference vs non-overlap of individual CIs

Two groups, both drawn from the same Normal distribution. $H_0: \mu_A = \mu_B$ is true by construction, so every rejection is a Type I error. We compare two decision rules on the same data: the proper one based on the CI of the difference $\bar X_A - \bar X_B$, and the common shortcut of building separate CIs for $\bar X_A$ and $\bar X_B$ and rejecting when the two CIs don't overlap. The shortcut uses an implicit larger critical value, so it is overly conservative and misses real effects.

data $X_i, Y_j \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ independent, so $H_0: \mu_A = \mu_B$ is true
proper test reject when 0 is outside CI of the difference. Requires $\lvert \bar X_A - \bar X_B \rvert \gt z \cdot \sqrt{\sigma_A^2/n_A + \sigma_B^2/n_B}$
non-overlap rule reject when CI of $\bar X_A$ and CI of $\bar X_B$ don't intersect. Requires $\lvert \bar X_A - \bar X_B \rvert \gt z \cdot (\mathrm{SE}_A + \mathrm{SE}_B)$. For equal SE this is $\sqrt 2$ times stricter

Both rules look reasonable on paper. Non-overlap is the visual shortcut that analysts reach for. With equal variances it requires a gap of $z \cdot (\mathrm{SE}_A + \mathrm{SE}_B) = z \cdot 2\,\mathrm{SE}$ to reject, while the proper test only needs $z \cdot \sqrt{\mathrm{SE}_A^2 + \mathrm{SE}_B^2} = z\sqrt 2 \cdot \mathrm{SE}$. Same data, a larger required gap, far fewer rejections. The red line settles well below $\alpha$. The test is too strict, which loses power on real effects.
