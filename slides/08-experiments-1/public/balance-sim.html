Randomization and balance, across many covariates

Each user carries six attributes: age, income, tenure, activity, country, device. We sample $n$ users and split into group A and B. With a random splitter, every attribute balances as $n$ grows. With a biased splitter (sort by income), the income panel splits cleanly, age also drifts because it correlates with income, and the rest stay balanced. The lesson: randomization handles attributes we forgot about; a broken splitter does not.

population 50,000 users with six attributes. Age $\sim \mathcal{N}(35, 10)$, income depends on age (correlated), tenure $\sim$ Exp, activity $\sim$ Beta, country and device categorical
random splitter bucket independent of every attribute
biased splitter sort by income, top half $\to$ A, bottom half $\to$ B
group A group B panel turns red when the gap exceeds 5% of the pooled mean (for continuous) or 5pp (for categorical)

Walk the slider with random: at $n = 50$ noticeable noise across panels. At $n = 1000$ all six panels overlap. Switch to biased: income panel splits cleanly into two non-overlapping halves, age shows a residual drift because age and income are correlated, country / device / tenure / activity stay balanced because the bias did not touch them directly. Randomization handles attributes we did not even know to check.
